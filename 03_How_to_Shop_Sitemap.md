2. Opening Section — How to Approach Furniture Shopping in Foshan
Purpose
Set expectations and explain that a productive visit or sourcing exercise begins with a clear idea of what the homeowner needs.
Content
Introduce a practical approach:
* Understand the scope of the home project.
* Identify the main furniture categories.
* Establish design preferences and quality expectations.
* Explore suitable manufacturers and showrooms.
* Compare products and specifications before making decisions.
Avoid suggesting that every visitor needs a formal sourcing plan or professional assistance.

3. Start with Your Home and Priorities
Purpose
Help visitors organize their requirements before exploring manufacturers.
Content themes
Encourage visitors to consider:
* Which rooms need furnishing.
* Which products are essential and which can wait.
* Preferred styles, materials, finishes, and dimensions.
* Quality expectations and intended use.
* Budget priorities across different furniture categories.
* Any practical constraints, such as room dimensions or access.
Explain that a clear brief makes it easier to compare relevant products and avoid spending time on unsuitable options.
A simple checklist may be useful here, but avoid creating an elaborate planning tool for the initial version.

4. Explore Broad-Range Manufacturers and Specialists
Purpose
Help visitors choose an appropriate starting point.
Content themes
Broad-range manufacturers
May be useful for visitors furnishing several rooms or seeking to coordinate multiple furniture categories.
Specialist manufacturers
May be useful for visitors who have a particular product in mind, such as outdoor furniture, chairs, stone tables, carpets, or mattresses.
Explain that these approaches are complementary. A homeowner may explore both broad-range manufacturers and specialists.
Link to the relevant sections of Furniture Catalogs where practical, without creating additional pages or navigation levels.

5. Use Catalogs as a Starting Point
Purpose
Explain what catalogs can and cannot tell visitors.
Content themes
* Catalogs help visitors understand a manufacturer's range and design direction.
* Product categories make it easier to narrow the search.
* Catalog images can help identify products worth investigating further.
* Images alone may not show every finish, dimension, material, or construction detail.
* Catalogs may contain older products or incomplete specifications.
Encourage visitors to confirm relevant details before making purchasing decisions.
Do not imply that every catalog on InsideFoshan represents the manufacturer's complete or current range.

6. Evaluate Products Beyond Appearance
Purpose
Help homeowners think critically about product quality and suitability.
Content themes
Depending on the product, visitors may want to understand:
* Materials and construction.
* Dimensions and proportions.
* Upholstery, finishes, and available options.
* Workmanship and consistency.
* Comfort, durability, and intended use.
* Packaging, transport, and installation considerations.
Emphasize that the relevant criteria vary by product category.
Avoid universal quality checklists that treat a mattress, dining chair, stone table, and outdoor sofa as if they require identical evaluation.

7. Understand the Difference Between a Showroom and a Catalog
Purpose
Set realistic expectations about product information.
Content themes
* Catalogs are useful for discovery and comparison.
* Showroom photography can provide a better sense of the products and their presentation.
* Seeing a product in person may help visitors assess scale, materials, finishes, and construction.
* A displayed product does not automatically confirm the availability of every configuration or option.
* Visitors should verify the details relevant to their own project.
Do not promise that every listed manufacturer offers complete showroom access or that every photographed item remains available.

8. Compare Options Before Committing
Purpose
Encourage deliberate decisions rather than choosing based on appearance alone.
Content themes
Visitors may wish to compare:
* Product specifications.
* Materials and construction.
* Available finishes and customization.
* Price quotations and what they include.
* Production lead times.
* Packaging and delivery arrangements.
* Warranty terms and after-sales arrangements.
* The clarity and reliability of communication.
Not every point applies to every product or purchase. Present these as considerations rather than universal requirements.
Avoid giving legal, customs, shipping, or import advice that has not been independently verified.

9. Consider How Much Help You Need
Purpose
Explain that visitors can explore independently or seek assistance, without pushing them toward a particular choice.
Content themes
Exploring independently
Visitors who are comfortable researching manufacturers, comparing options, and communicating directly may prefer to manage the process themselves.
Getting assistance
Some visitors may prefer help narrowing down options, identifying suitable manufacturers, coordinating enquiries, or navigating unfamiliar aspects of the market.
Explain the distinction neutrally. Do not claim that professional sourcing is necessary for everyone or that using an agent guarantees a better outcome.
A Need More Help? action can open the contact modal for visitors who want to discuss their needs.

10. Common Mistakes to Avoid
Purpose
Provide a short, useful checklist of avoidable problems.
Suggested themes
* Choosing based on photographs alone.
* Assuming similar-looking products have identical materials or construction.
* Comparing quotations without checking what is included.
* Failing to confirm dimensions and specifications.
* Assuming that catalog information is complete or current.
* Leaving delivery, installation, or after-sales questions until too late.
* Making decisions without considering the overall requirements of the home.
Keep this section concise. Avoid an alarmist tone or unsupported claims about common industry misconduct.
